Purpose

Establish a clear and systematic approach for identifying and managing lithium-ion related incidents.

Lithium-ion cells contain large amounts of energy. When that energy is released in an uncontrolled manner due to improper use, storage, or physical damage, it may cause a lithium-ion incident. Lithium-ion incidents pose unique hazards, including combustion, explosion, and toxic gas release. It is paramount that those working with lithium-ion cells review this article to enable a rapid incident response.

WARNING: Only perform actions described within this article when safe to do so. If the event is too large or too dangerous to approach, evacuate and contact emergency services.

Scope

The identification, containment, and post-event handling of lithium-ion related incidents.

Form Factors

  • Lithium-ion and lithium metal cells and batteries.
  • Batteries alone, packed with equipment, or contained in equipment.

Statuses

  • New, Used, EOL, and DDR (Damaged, Defective, or Recalled).
  • DDR includes batteries with potential for dangerous and rapid combustion or short circuit.

Activities Covered

  • Incident management.
  • Post-incident management. 

Definitions

Term Definition
Cell / Battery Includes both lithium-ion and lithium metal chemistries.
Equipment Device or machine powered by lithium cells or batteries.
Damaged / Defective / Recall (DDR) Batteries identified as defective or with potential for dangerous evolution of combustion, or short circuit.
EOL End of Life.
SOH State of Health.
LIR Lithium-ion Incident Report.
Thermal runaway Rapid and uncontrolled rise in temperature that can result in fire or explosion.
Calendar age Amount of time elapsed since the manufacture date of the cell.
Cycle Cell degradation caused by repeated charging events.

Roles and Responsibilities

Personnel Working With Lithium-Ion Cells

  • Identify DDR cells exhibiting an incident condition.
  • Take response actions only when it is safe to do so.
  • Complete a Lithium-ion Incident Report (LIR) after an incident.

Emergency Services

  • Contacted for any fire, explosion, or leak incident, and for any medical exposure.
  • Informed of the situation, including the materials involved.

ITAM Lead

  • Retains a completed copy of the LIR in the organization's SharePoint.

Medical Professionals

  • Provide the authoritative decision regarding medical treatment. The guidance in this article does not replace their direction.

Identifying Incident Conditions

NOTICE: If a lithium-ion cell exhibits any of the conditions below, immediately classify it as DDR and follow DDR handling procedures. This list is not exhaustive.

Precondition

Understand the following before responding to a lithium-ion event.

  • Even after extinguishing a lithium-ion fire, there is a risk of reignition.
  • Medical emergencies can develop from even short-term exposure to a lithium-ion incident.
  • Fire extinguishers often do not have enough thermal mass to extinguish lithium-ion fires. Water is the recommended method for fire suppression.
  • Know the location of the nearest emergency exit, alarm, and fire suppression equipment.

Overcharging

Most modern lithium systems have overcharge protection. However, these systems can fail, allowing the overcharge of lithium-ion cells. This causes higher resistance, leading to thermal instability. Never reuse an overcharged lithium battery.

  • Signs: hot to the touch, bulging, emitting an unusual odor or chemical smell.

Degradation

Lithium-ion cells degrade over time, leading to poor SOH. A cell's SOH is calculated based on the cell's calendar age and number of cycles.

  • Signs: bulging cells, reduced battery life.

Physical Damage

Punctured, deformed, and/or otherwise damaged cells can catch fire or explode, potentially causing an incident with secondary ignition sources.

  • Signs: visual damage, leaking cells, emitting an unusual odor or chemical smell.

Thermal Runaway

Rapid and uncontrolled rise in temperature that can result in fire or explosion.

  • Signs: bulging, hissing, smoking, rapid rise in temperature.

Fire, Explosion, and Leak Management

WARNING: The actions in this section should ONLY be performed if it is safe to do so.

Managing Fire Incidents

  • Contact emergency services. Inform emergency services personnel of the situation, including the materials involved.
  • Keep unauthorized personnel away.
  • Spray water or foam onto the base of the lithium-ion fire.
  • Identify any subsequent fires developing in proximity to the initial fire.
  • Move undamaged containers away from the area around the fire.
  • Ventilate the area and stay upwind.

Managing Explosion Incidents

  • Contact emergency services. Inform emergency services personnel of the situation, including the materials involved.
  • Keep unauthorized personnel away.
  • Spray water or foam onto the base of the lithium-ion fire.
  • Identify any subsequent fires developing in proximity to the initial explosion.
  • Move undamaged containers away from the area around the fire.
  • Ventilate the area and stay upwind.

Managing Leak Incidents

  • Eliminate all possible ignition sources from the immediate area.
  • Do not touch or walk through leaked material.
  • Contact emergency medical services if needed. Inform emergency services personnel of the situation, including the materials involved.
  • Keep unauthorized personnel away.
  • Absorb lithium-ion leaks with sand or non-combustible material.
  • Remove and isolate contaminated clothing.
  • Place leaking cells and contaminated material in inert material containers.

Evacuation

If the incident is too big or too dangerous, execute the following evacuation protocol:

  • Immediately exit through the closest emergency exit.
  • Immediately contact local emergency response teams and deploy emergency alarms.
  • Congregate upwind and begin basic triage on those affected.

Medical Management

WARNING: Lithium-ion incidents pose health risks that can lead to bodily harm or death. Always consult a medical professional for an authoritative decision regarding medical treatment. The health effects and treatment methods listed here are not exhaustive and should not be used to replace treatment guidance from professional medical personnel.

Health Effects

  • Lithium-ion cells can produce toxic fumes that can cause dizziness or asphyxiation.
  • Lithium-ion cells can produce hydrogen fluoride gas, a powerful corrosive and contact poison.
  • Contact with lithium-ion electrolytes may irritate skin, eyes, and the respiratory tract.
  • Lithium-ion fires can produce third-degree burns.

First Aid

Always contact a medical professional immediately after a lithium-ion incident, regardless of whether you believe you need medical attention. When contacting medical personnel, ensure they are aware of the materials involved. The actions below are recommended during and after a lithium-ion incident, based on the type of injury.

Injury Type First Aid Actions
Dizziness and/or unconsciousness Move the victim to fresh upwind air, if it can be done safely. If the victim is unconscious, check airway and pulse, and perform CPR if necessary. Contact emergency medical personnel.
Contact with lithium-ion electrolytes Remove and isolate contaminated clothing. Immediately flush skin or eyes with running water for at least 20 minutes. Contact emergency medical personnel.
Contact with lithium-ion gases, including hydrogen fluoride Move the victim to fresh upwind air, if it can be done safely. Immediately contact emergency medical personnel for medical attention.

Evidence and Records

For incidents involving lithium-ion cells, document the type of battery and the extent of the incident in the Lithium-ion Incident Report (LIR). Retain the document for internal reference.

  • A printed copy is retained by the ITAM Lead in a secure location.
  • All LIRs are stored electronically in a dedicated LIR folder within the organization's SharePoint site.

Escalation

Escalate immediately in the following situations.

  • Fire, explosion, or leak involving a lithium-ion cell: 
    • Contact emergency services and inform them of the materials involved.

  • The incident is too big or too dangerous to manage:
    • Deploy emergency alarms, contact local emergency response teams, and evacuate.

  • If any person was exposed to fumes, gases, electrolytes, or fire:
    • Contact emergency medical personnel.

  • Reignition after an apparently extinguished fire:
    • Treat as a new fire incident and contact emergency services.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is water safe for lithium-ion fires?

Yes. Water works as a fire extinguishing medium because the lithium inside these batteries is a lithium salt electrolyte, not pure lithium metal. Pure lithium is reactive with water, while lithium salts are non-reactive.

Where should I store the LIR?

All LIRs should be stored electronically in a dedicated LIR folder within the organization's SharePoint site.
