Purpose

NOTICE: Review the Lithium-Ion Event Response SOP in tandem with this article.

Lithium-ion cells must be stored based on their lifecycle classification. This article states the storage, preparation, and packaging requirements for lithium-ion batteries, with a focus on devices identified as Defective, Damaged, or Recalled.

  • Establish the environmental and physical conditions under which DDR cells are stored while awaiting disposition or disposal.
  • Establish the sequence for preparing, packaging, containing, and sealing a DDR cell for disposal.
  • Provide a checklist that confirms each disposal preparation requirement was met.

Scope

Storage and disposal of lithium-ion cells within the organization.

In Scope

  • Lithium-ion lifecycle classifications.
  • DDR disposition guidance.
  • Storage environment conditions and restrictions.

Out of Scope

  • Response to an active lithium-ion incident, including fire, explosion, and leak management. See the Lithium-Ion Event Response article.

Definitions

The following terms are used throughout this article.

Term Definition
Cell / Battery Includes both lithium-ion and lithium metal chemistries.
DDR Defective, Damaged, or Recalled. DDR cells are batteries that may present an elevated safety risk due to damage, defects, or recall status, including the potential for short-circuiting, overheating, thermal runaway, fire, or rapid combustion.
Equipment A device or machine powered by lithium cells or batteries.
EOL End of Life.
SOC State of Charge.
SOH State of Health.
Known-good cell A cell that has not been classified as DDR and is therefore stored separately from DDR stock.

Roles and Responsibilities

Trained and Authorized Personnel

  • Only personnel who have received the appropriate safety and handling training may move, package, or otherwise handle DDR batteries.
  • Access to designated DDR battery storage areas is restricted to trained and authorized personnel.
  • Confirm that containers are sealed and labeled per disposal requirements before pickup.

Designated Disposal Personnel

  • Dispose of batteries in accordance with applicable local and federal regulations.

Preconditions

Confirm the following before beginning any disposal preparation activity.

  • The chemistry has been identified.
  • The cell has been classified, and its DDR status is known.
  • The person performing the work has completed the appropriate safety training and is authorized to handle DDR batteries.
  • The storage room contains proper fire suppression equipment.
  • Required materials are on hand: polyimide tape, anti-static bags, vermiculite, and an approved non-combustible metal disposal container.
WARNING: If a cell shows signs of an active or developing event, follow the protocols outlined in the Lithium-Ion Event Response article instead.

Lifecycle Storage Requirements

Batteries awaiting disposition or use should be stored under controlled environmental conditions that minimize the risk of damage, short-circuiting, overheating, or other battery-related incidents.

Battery Charge Requirements

  • Reduce cells to ≤ 15% SOC (State of Charge) prior to packaging, when feasible.

Storage Environment Requirements

  • Temperature: 50°F - 65°F.
  • Humidity: 10% -  40%.
  • Do not store batteries near fire exits, emergency egress routes, or evacuation pathways.
  • Ensure the storage room contains proper fire suppression equipment.
REMINDER: Review the Lithium-Ion Event Response article for further fire readiness guidance.

A3. Storage Location and Segregation

  • Maintain physical separation between known-good cells and DDR cells to prevent accidental mixing, handling, or reuse.
  • Restrict access to designated DDR battery storage areas to trained and authorized personnel.

DDR Disposal Preparation

Complete the following steps for each cell being prepared for disposal. Each battery should be handled and packaged individually.

Step 1. Locate the Battery for Disposal

  • Identify the specific battery you intend to package.
  • Handle it carefully and avoid bending, puncturing, or crushing it.

Step 2. Detach Accessories

  • Detach any cables, screws, brackets, or external hardware that are not permanently fixed to the battery.
  • Do not attempt to remove components that require prying, cutting, puncturing, or otherwise disturbing the battery enclosure.

Step 3. Protect Terminals

  • Apply polyimide tape to the battery terminals to prevent short circuits.
  • Verify that conductive surfaces are fully covered before proceeding with packaging.

Step 4. Package and Secure the Battery

  • Place each battery in its own anti-static bag.
  • Fold the anti-static bag around the battery.
  • Seal the bag using polyimide tape.
  • Do not package multiple batteries within the same anti-static bag.

Step 5. Load the Disposal Container

  • Prepare an approved non-combustible metal disposal container with a minimum 10-inch base layer of vermiculite.
  • Place the individually packaged battery onto the vermiculite base layer.
  • Cover the battery with vermiculite so it is fully surrounded with a minimum 1-inch layer on all sides, ensuring it is well cushioned and cannot shift, collide, or be damaged during movement.

Step 6. Seal the Disposal Container

  • Securely seal the metal container and label it per disposal requirements.
  • Repeat the preceding steps until the container is full, or until the barrel is set for pickup.
  • Do not leave DDR batteries in the barrel for more than 6 months at a time.

DDR Disposal Checklist

Review the DDR checklist when preparing cells for disposal.

  • ☐  Chemistry identified (Li-ion).
  • ☐  Status identified (DDR).
  • ☐  Battery charge at 15% or less, if possible.
  • ☐  All screws and cables removed from battery cell, if applicable.
  • ☐  Terminals protected with polyimide tape.
  • ☐  Battery packaged in anti-static bags.
  • ☐  Packaged battery placed in a metal barrel containing at least 10 inches of vermiculite.
  • ☐  Packaged battery is fully cushioned and cannot reasonably shift, collide, or become damaged during movement.
  • ☐  Stored in a cool, dry environment and away from ignition sources.
  • ☐  Designated personnel disposed of batteries per local and federal regulations.

Escalation

Stop the procedure and escalate in the following situations.

  • A cell shows signs of an active or developing lithium-ion incident event.
  • A cell cannot be packaged without prying, cutting, or puncturing the enclosure.
  • Storage environment conditions fail to meet the standards.
  • A DDR disposition barrel is approaching the 6-month dwell limit and no pickup has been scheduled.
